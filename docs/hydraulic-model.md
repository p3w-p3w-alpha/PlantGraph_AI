# Hydraulic model and operating assumptions

This document covers the preserved **Atlas** regression case. The source-derived Lower Fox case has a different process order, finite-tank inflow/outflow controller and source interlocks; its facts, assumptions and calculations are documented in [lower-fox-model.md](lower-fox-model.md). Values below must never be assigned to that case.

This document describes the implemented station in `backend/app/simulation_service.py` and the exported `data/epanet/station.inp`. It is a representative model with deliberately documented parameters. Its readings are generated by EPANET; they are not field measurements or a calibration against BATADAL.

## Computational model

WNTR 1.5 builds an EPANET 2.2 network for each operating point. `EpanetSimulator` solves a duration-zero hydraulic snapshot with the Hazen–Williams pipe headloss model, zero fixed junction demands, a finite storage tank, two parallel pump links, a filter resistance, a throttling valve, an isolation link, and a downstream fixed-head reservoir.

EPANET solves conservation of mass at junctions and the pressure/head relationships of connected links. It determines the operating flow from the pump curve, losses, tank head, and downstream head. The outlet is not a fixed-flow demand: increased resistance can therefore reduce delivery.

Tank inventory is advanced between snapshots by the service's explicit mass-balance integration. This is a sequence of quasi-steady operating points. It is not an EPANET extended-period run, water-hammer simulation, fluid-particle solver, CFD calculation, or model of filtration chemistry.

The language model does not calculate hydraulic readings. It receives observations and engineering context and proposes diagnostic hypotheses. What-if numbers are obtained from another EPANET solve.

## Physical and graph mappings

The investigation graph represents pumps and valves as selectable equipment nodes. EPANET represents those same pieces of equipment as hydraulic **links**. Their mapping is explicit; graphical asset positions are display coordinates and are not physical elevations or pipe lengths.

| Graph asset | Hydraulic representation |
| --- | --- |
| T-101 | Tank node T-101 |
| J-SUCTION | Junction SUCTION |
| P-101A | Pump link SUCTION → DISCHARGE |
| P-101B | Parallel pump link SUCTION → DISCHARGE |
| J-DISCHARGE | Junction DISCHARGE |
| F-101 | Pipe link FILTER-IN → FILTER-OUT with variable minor loss |
| V-101 | TCV link FILTER-OUT → VALVE-OUT |
| V-102 | Pipe link ISOLATION-IN → OUTLET, switched open/closed |
| OUTLET | Reservoir node OUTLET, fixed head 20 m |

Supporting pipes account for the active-path length:

| Link | Endpoints | Length |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| SUCTION-PIPE | T-101 → SUCTION | 8 m |
| DISCHARGE-PIPE | DISCHARGE → FILTER-IN | 20 m |
| F-101 equivalent | FILTER-IN → FILTER-OUT | 0.1 m |
| DELIVERY-PIPE | VALVE-OUT → ISOLATION-IN | 25 m |
| V-102 equivalent | ISOLATION-IN → OUTLET | 26.9 m |

Both pumps connect directly between the common headers; independent branch-pipe losses and check-valve hardware are not separately parameterized. EPANET pump behavior prevents a pump from acting as an unrestricted reverse-flow pipe.

## Instruments and unit conversion

WNTR stores quantities internally in SI units, including flow in m³/s and pressure head in metres. The exported INP uses LPS conventions; WNTR handles that file conversion.

| Instrument / displayed value | Hydraulic source and conversion |
| --- | --- |
| LT-101 depth | Current service tank depth, m |
| Tank percentage | `depth / 6 × 100`; 1 m trip level is 16.67%, not 0% |
| PT-101 | FILTER-IN pressure head × 0.0980665 bar/m |
| PT-102 | FILTER-OUT pressure head × 0.0980665 bar/m |
| Filter ΔP | `PT-101 − PT-102`, calculated from the same snapshot |
| FT-101 / delivery / flow | V-102 link flow × 3600, m³/h |
| Pump outflow | Sum of P-101A and P-101B link flow, used for tank inventory |
| Leak loss | VALVE-OUT junction demand from its emitter × 3600, m³/h |
| Pump head | DISCHARGE head minus SUCTION head when enabled; reported as zero when stopped or interlocked |

The filter pressure taps are at equal elevation and immediately bracket its equivalent link. Their pressure difference includes its minor loss and short-pipe friction; it does not include the control valve or the delivery piping.

`pump_head_m` is a header differential, not shaft power, efficiency, or evidence of actual flow. A running pump with a closed outlet can be at shutoff. A speed too low to overcome the downstream head can produce zero delivery even though the selected motor is designated running.

Displayed delivery/leak flows below 0.001 m³/h in magnitude and pump outflow below 0.000001 m³/s are treated as zero to remove EPANET closed-link numerical residuals. This is a numerical tolerance, not injected measurement noise.

## Resistance, speed, health, and leak relationships

For the filter, EPANET applies the standard minor-loss relationship:

`head loss = K × velocity² / (2g)`

The filter's `K` is `filter_resistance`. Increasing it shifts the system curve and changes the pump operating point. The pressure loss also depends on flow; differential pressure alone is not proof of one particular failure.

For V-101, with fractional opening `o = valve_open_pct / 100`:

`K_valve = 5 + 200 × (1 − o)²`

At exactly 0% the valve link is closed. This is an explicit illustrative characteristic, not a manufacturer Cv/Kv curve. Thirty percent opening does not mean thirty percent delivery.

EPANET applies the variable-speed pump affinity relationship using speed ratio `n`:

`H_n(Q) = n² × H_1(Q/n)`

P-101A health additionally scales its curve's head ordinates by `pump_health_pct / 100`. P-101B retains the healthy head curve. The health parameter does not establish a particular physical damage mechanism or motor efficiency.

The leak is an EPANET emitter at VALVE-OUT:

`Q_leak = C_leak × pressure_head^0.5`

`C_leak = 0.0015 × leak_severity / 100`

The normal positive-pressure leak case obeys `pump outflow = delivery + emitter outflow` within solver tolerance. The fixed-head downstream reservoir can also supply reverse flow under some nonstandard operator combinations; it is a hydraulic boundary, not a check valve or consumer-only sink.

## Scenarios

Scenario severity `s` is normalized to 0–1; the API default is 0.7. Each scenario first restores the clean equipment controls and then applies the modification below. It preserves the current tank inventory and simulation clock, except for the explicit low-tank setup.

| Scenario | Modification |
| --- | --- |
| Baseline | Clean controls; does **not** refill the tank |
| Filter clogging | `K_filter = 10 + 100s`; default 80 |
| Pump degradation | `health_A = 100 − 70s` percent; default 51% |
| Valve restriction | `opening = max(0, 100 − 95s)` percent; default 33.5% |
| Leak | `leak_severity = 100s` percent; default 70% |
| Low tank | `depth = 1 + 0.4(1 − s)` m; default 1.12 m |

At the initial 4 m depth, default filter clogging reduces delivery to approximately 52.48 m³/h and increases filter ΔP to 1.377 bar. Default pump degradation reduces delivery to approximately 40.92 m³/h. These are solver outputs for the stated assumptions, not fitted scenario targets.

Selecting P-101B restores delivery after P-101A degradation because it selects an independent healthy pump curve. The same swap does not remove F-101 resistance: both pump branches discharge into the same filter. A useful investigation compares these interventions rather than assuming any pump change repairs any low-flow symptom.

## Time, inventory, and operating states

The service starts paused, at 30× speed. Operator speed range is 0.1–600×. It uses a monotonic wall clock and generates displayed timestamps as the reset UTC origin plus accumulated simulation seconds.

Paused playback freezes the simulation clock and inventory; it does not issue a pump-stop command. The saved hydraulic operating point can therefore have nonzero delivery and a running duty-pump status while paused. A visual animation must separately honor the `playing` flag. The standby pump has zero commanded speed (`equipment[standby].value = 0`) and no active flow contribution. For the frozen filter-only investigation, with no leak, total pumped flow equals delivery within solver tolerance.

While playing, state polling advances the model. Operator pause, speed, scenario, and control events first settle elapsed time with the **old** settings, then apply the new event. This prevents a new pump setting from being used retroactively for the preceding interval. AI reads and pure what-if comparisons call `get_state(advance=False)` and do not advance the session.

For each integration substep of at most 30 simulated seconds:

`V_used = Q_pump × Δt`

`depth_next = depth_current − V_used / tank_area`

There is no inflow. If a substep would cross the minimum depth, it is shortened to the available usable volume. The next snapshot is solved at the updated tank depth. At 1 m, within a 0.00001 m numerical tolerance, pumping is interlocked and playback stops. Reset defines a fresh initial-value problem: 4 m tank level, clean controls, elapsed time zero, paused state, and cleared history.

Rapid polls coalesce intervals below 50 ms without discarding their pending elapsed time. Up to 60 real seconds are caught up per poll; longer browser suspension intentionally does not skip unbounded simulated time. At most 300 state/history snapshots are retained in memory. This is a single local demonstration session, not durable multi-user SCADA storage.

Equipment status is derived from controls and explicit rules:

- The selected pump is running when speed is positive and the low-level interlock is clear; the other is standby. Zero requested speed stops the selected pump. Low-low level trips it.
- V-101 is closed at 0%, throttled below 95%, otherwise open. V-102 is open or closed.
- Tank, filter, and outlet warning states come from the observed alarm rules below.
- No automatic duty changeover, restart sequence, backwash procedure, or real equipment command is assumed.

## Alarm rules and evidence

| Alarm | Explicit rule |
| --- | --- |
| Low tank warning | Depth below 1.5 m but above trip level |
| Low-low tank trip | Depth at 1 m within level tolerance |
| High filter differential | PT-101 minus PT-102 exceeds 0.80 bar |
| Low delivery | Delivery below 78% of the initial clean 4 m baseline |
| Unaccounted flow | Emitter loss exceeds 0.5 m³/h; evidence shows pump/delivery difference |
| Solver failure | No new converged hydraulic result |

Thresholds are demonstration rules, not utility alarm settings. The low-delivery reference is the initial baseline and does not automatically adjust for tank drawdown.

On a solver failure the service retains the last valid readings, marks `solver_ok=false`, emits a solver alarm, and pauses playback. Those readings are stale evidence, not successful predictions for the new controls. The initial model must solve successfully before the service starts.

## What-if contract and limitations

A what-if comparison uses the same current tank depth, downstream head, and timestamp for baseline and prediction. It validates a copy of the requested controls, runs an independent hydraulic snapshot, and returns per-variable deltas. It does not advance time, alter controls, modify history, or execute a change on the live session. It is an instantaneous operating-point comparison, not a forecast of future depletion.

The P&ID establishes equipment identity and candidate connectivity; it does not establish these numerical parameters or a surveyed 3D arrangement. A reviewed graph must match the supported station template and have a parameter binding before it can be represented by this solver. An unrelated uploaded drawing remains an extraction/investigation artifact rather than being silently simulated as this station.

Known exclusions are water quality, actual filtration performance, water hammer, cavitation/NPSH, motor power and efficiency, detailed pump branch valves, automatic sequencing, sensor noise/delay, field calibration, real PLC communication, and the exact reproduction of BATADAL time series.
